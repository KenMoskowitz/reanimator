# DANA FIGHTS AN EMPTY SATURDAY

Project: Acquire Clients. Audio: both. Runtime: 60 seconds. Status: draft, not locked.

## Client avatar

- Who: owner of a local product or service business under $1M a year, one to eight people, owner decides and does the work.
- Daily pain: her ads look like everyone else's, no time to write them, no budget for an agency, her last post reached eleven people.
- Wants: a full day of customers on days she picks, and ads she is proud to show her family.
- Really buying: proof that building the business with her own hands was not a mistake.
- Trigger: a slow week, a bill coming due, or a competitor ad that looks better than hers.
- Objections: "I got burned by an agency." "AI ads look fake." "Another subscription?"
- Budget signal: small amounts, often. Needs a clear monthly price.
- Identity: the maker. Trusts what she can touch, not jargon.

## 00:00 - 00:10 HOOK

INT. DANA'S DONUT WINDOW, BACK KITCHEN - 11:52 P.M. FRIDAY

A 1978 Hobart mixer thumps on a steel table. Twenty dozen cinnamon rolls sit on rolling racks, glaze going dull. One fluorescent tube buzzes, one is dead. The room smells like burnt sugar and floor cleaner. DANA (40s, flour to the elbows, hairnet crooked) stares at her phone on the counter. A smoke detector chirps once.

ON-SCREEN: 20 DOZEN ROLLS. NOBODY KNOWS WE'RE OPEN.

[VISIBLE GOAL] Sell every roll by noon tomorrow, or Monday's rent check bounces.

DANA (V.O.): I have twenty dozen cinnamon rolls, twelve hours, and nobody knows I'm open.

Her phone shows her last ad. "Reached 11 people."

DANA: Nine of them are my cousins.

The front window behind her goes dark for a second. A shape shuffles past outside, then is gone. Dana turns.

DANA: Who's out there?

SOUND: mixer thump, one slow scrape on the glass. Cut.

## 00:10 - 00:35 FORESHADOW + VALUE DISCOVERY

INT. BACK KITCHEN, PREP TABLE - 12:40 A.M.

A laptop with fourteen tabs open sits in a drift of flour. Dana types "BEST ROLLS IN TOWN!!! COME ON DOWN!!!" and stares at it. She deletes the whole line.

DANA (V.O.): I know dough. I don't know how to talk about dough.

She props one roll on a sheet pan and fires the phone flash. On the screen the roll is gray, flat, wet-cardboard gray.

DANA: That looks like a sponge.

ON-SCREEN: 1:14 A.M.

EXT. STRIP MALL LOT - 1:14 A.M.

One sodium light buzzes over an empty lot. On one side, a dark vape shop. On the other, a dead RadioShack sign missing half its letters. Dana holds a roll up to her window for better light. The neon sign above her reads OPE. The last letter is dead. Behind her reflection in the glass, shapes drift across the lot, slow, heads down. She does not see them. She is looking at her phone. It is still gray.

INT. BACK KITCHEN, REGISTER - 2:30 A.M.

A yellow sticky note is taped to the register in her nephew's block handwriting: "Try the zombies. $97. - Mo." Dana crumples it and throws it at the trash. It misses and lands on her sleeve.

SOUND: smoke detector chirp, now every few seconds. The mixer slows.

## 00:35 - 00:45 ALL IS LOST + SOLUTION TRIGGER

INT. BACK KITCHEN, WALK-IN COOLER DOOR - 3:12 A.M.

Dana deletes the post. The mixer stops. The smoke detector dies mid-chirp. Silence. She slides down the cooler door onto a flour sack. The rolls cool. The glaze goes flat and matte.

DANA (V.O.): Maybe the rolls aren't the problem. Maybe I'm just a baker who can't talk.

She picks the sticky note off her sleeve and reads it. She opens a form on her phone and types one sentence with two flour thumbs. She reads it out loud as she types.

DANA: Cinnamon rolls. Saturday. Gone by noon.

ON-SCREEN: SENT. 3:15 A.M. BACK IN 3 HOURS.

SOUND: one soft chime. A long breath out.

## 00:45 - 00:55 CLIMAX

INT. BACK KITCHEN - 6:04 A.M.

Her phone lights up. A new ad: one cinnamon roll, steam curling off cracked glaze, golden window light across the frosting. Under it, five plain words: "Warm. Sticky. Gone by noon." Dana stares.

EXT. STRIP MALL LOT - 6:58 A.M. BLUE DAWN

Breath fogs in the cold. Shapes pour across the lot toward her one lit window. Dozens. Hoods up. Heads down. Arms hanging. A low groan rolls across the asphalt. Dana grabs a rolling pin.

DANA: No. No, no, no.

The bell above the door jingles. A groggy man in a puffy jacket shuffles in, eyes on his phone.

CUSTOMER: You the cinnamon roll place? Saw your ad. Give me six.

DANA: Six?

CUSTOMER: I have a mother-in-law.

Behind him the line runs out the door, past the vape shop, and around the corner. These are not zombies. They are hungry.

## 00:55 - 01:00 TRANSFORMATION + CTA

INT. DANA'S DONUT WINDOW, FRONT COUNTER - 11:41 A.M.

Empty racks. Flour on her cheek. Dana hands over the last bag and flips the sign. The dead letter above her flickers, catches, and glows: OPEN. Her phone buzzes. A text from Mo: "Told you."

DANA (V.O.): If your shop is quiet, you don't need better rolls. You need better ads.

DANA (V.O.): Ad Zombies Express. Copy and pictures for small businesses. Starting at ninety-seven dollars a month.
