# LONG-FORM SEQUENCE STORY STRUCTURE

**MC = Main Character**

The original framework organizes a dramatic sequence into ten one-minute story beats.

The filmmaker may rehearse the dramatic action first, then capture coverage with multiple angles and establishing coverage when entering an important new location or event.

---

# PER-SEQUENCE TIMELINE

## 00:00–01:00 - STATUS QUO

## 01:00–02:00 - THE CATALYST

## 02:00–03:00 - REFUSAL OF THE CALL

## 03:00–04:00 - ADVENTURE BEGINS / POINT OF NO RETURN

## 04:00–05:00 - MIDPOINT

## 05:00–06:00 - APPROACHING INMOST CAVE

## 06:00–07:00 - ALL IS LOST

## 07:00–08:00 - FINAL PUSH

## 08:00–09:00 - CLIMAX / SEIZING THE SWORD

## 09:00–10:00 - AFTERMATH / RETURN WITH THE ELIXIR

---

# INTRO TEASER

The teaser:

- Hooks the audience
- Reveals the genre
- Uses strong set pieces
- Runs approximately one minute
- Appears before Status Quo

It may involve the MC or may focus elsewhere.

The goal is impact.

---

# SEQUENCE 01 - LIFE BEFORE THE TWIST / STATUS QUO

Possible beats:

- Character's daily routine
- First-act goal
- Show where the character reveals emotional state
- Show they may not be handling life well
- Point toward possible relationships
- Introduce brewing antagonist conflict
- Show hero's superpower / talent
- Show hero's kryptonite / weakness
- Introduce the routine killer around the 10% mark

---

# SEQUENCE 02 - ROUTINE KILLER RESPONSE

## CRITICAL RULE

The MC's **VISIBLE GOAL** must be established here.

It is the mission that organizes the rest of the story through the end of Sequence 8.

Possible beats:

- Hero responds to routine killer.
- Create "Should I stay or should I go?" tension.
- Hero initially refuses.
- Give the hero fears and obligations.
- Give reasons to leave.
- Give reasons to stay.
- Create an obstacle the MC overcomes to move forward.
- Show preparation.
- Create obstacles delaying Act 2.
- Shift focus from simply showing the flaw to an activity that gets the character into trouble.
- Trigger a complication.
- Let someone challenge the hero.
- Let the MC go somewhere they should not.
- Let the MC do something questionable.
- Use a disastrous mistake or slip of the tongue.
- Create a major obstacle at the end of Act 1.
- Suggest a way out.
- Establish a new visible goal with strategy.

---

# ACT 1 PLOT TWIST

The hero realizes they are about to go on an adventure.

The twist forces the protagonist out of the comfort zone.

The first step can be:

- Emotional
- Geographic
- Social
- Romantic
- Professional
- Supernatural

The important thing is that the old state cannot simply continue unchanged.

---

# SEQUENCE 03 - THE ADVENTURE / THE PLAN

Reflect the Visible Goal.

Show the character developing and attempting a plan.

Possible beats:

- Learn the new environment.
- Train.
- Gather a team.
- Investigate.
- Track.
- Test a plan.
- Let the plan partially work.
- Let it fail.
- Give a supporting character a useful skill.
- Have a supporting character raise an important question.
- Let a supporting character test the MC emotionally.
- Push the MC toward new action.
- Suggest antagonist pressure.

End on a beat that makes the audience wonder what happens next.

---

# SEQUENCE 04 - COMPLICATION / MIDPOINT

The MC may become absorbed in immediate complications and temporarily lose focus on the end goal.

Possible beats:

- Become too invested in the current plan.
- Discover clues revealing a larger problem.
- Make it impossible to return to the former status quo.
- Increase audience investment.

At the midpoint use at least one:

1. MC engages in riskier activity.
2. Antagonist makes a major move.
3. Emotional involvement with a supporting character deepens.
4. Pay off something mundane from Act 1 / early Act 2.
5. Establish an ultimatum or ticking clock.

---

# SEQUENCE 05 - INTENSIFICATION

No more setup.

Let the MC apply something learned.

Possible beats:

- Apply a new skill.
- Take a risk.
- Create a fight or chase unlike earlier action.
- Deliver a fright.
- Escalate romance.
- Deepen supporting character story.
- Show intense trouble.
- Move antagonist closer.
- Let antagonist fight harder.
- Build a trap.
- Revisit the hero's lifelong dream.

---

# SEQUENCE 06 - APPROACHING COLLAPSE / ALL IS LOST

This is strongly emotional.

Previous choices threaten to break the journey.

Possible beats:

- Greater risk creates greater chance of failure.
- The hero's flaw returns.
- Kryptonite pays off.
- Personal doubt rises.
- The plan collapses.
- All is lost.

The lower the emotional point, the greater the contrast available for the eventual victory.

---

# SEQUENCE 07 - FINAL PUSH

Something makes the MC refocus.

Possible beats:

- Pay off a mundane earlier line.
- Pay off an object.
- Pay off an action.
- Pay off a prior event.
- Create an "A-Ha" moment.
- Trigger the correct final action.
- Show the audience the Act 3 strategy.
- Supporting character makes a consequential choice.
- Antagonist reveals one final trick.

---

# SEQUENCE 08 - FINAL SHOWDOWN

Pay off:

- Minor characters
- Supporting characters
- Hero's superpower
- Learned skill
- Knowledge gathered on the journey
- Emotional growth

The hero's original flaw may be reworked into a useful quality.

Resolve the Visible Goal.

Show how events changed:

- MC
- Allies
- Antagonist
- World

Create closure.

Then tag the sequence with a small final beat suggesting the future.

Cliffhanger where appropriate.
