# HOOK PSYCHOLOGY + SIX HOOK ARCHETYPES

This document is a structured extraction of the hook-training source.

---

# WHY HOOKS MATTER

The hook is treated as the highest-leverage portion of a short-form video.

Its job is to stop the scroll and create enough curiosity for the viewer to continue.

---

# THE PSYCHOLOGY OF GREAT HOOKS

Hooks create a **curiosity loop**.

Think of it as a rabbit hole in the viewer's brain.

The strongest way to create this loop is often **contrast**.

The viewer begins believing:

**A**

Then the hook introduces:

**B**

The distance between A and B creates contrast.

More meaningful contrast → more curiosity.

The challenge is that the contrast needs to register within approximately five seconds.

The six hook formats below are different ways to create that contrast.

---

# 1. THE FORTUNE TELLER

## Core Idea

Position the present against a possible future.

Create curiosity around how the world, category, product, or behavior may change.

## Contrast

Current reality → surprising future reality.

## Tactical Steps

### A. Establish current reality.

What does the viewer currently know or expect?

### B. Identify the future change.

How might the subject transform that reality?

### C. Tease the future.

Frame a question or statement suggesting the new future.

## Useful For

- Breaking news
- Product innovation
- Technology
- Trends
- Authority around the future of a category

---

# 2. THE EXPERIMENTER

## Core Idea

Demonstrate how something works through a real test, experiment, or live example.

The tone is peer-to-peer:

> I tried this. Watch what happened.

## Contrast

Old method → tested new method.

## Tactical Steps

### A. Establish the pain/problem.

### B. Show that you tried a different method/tool/process.

### C. Demonstrate why it works differently or better.

## Visual Strength

The hook can sometimes demonstrate the subject directly rather than merely talking about it.

## Useful For

- Product demos
- New tools
- Frameworks
- Challenges
- B2B demos
- Real-world construction or process videos

---

# 3. THE TEACHER

## Core Idea

Instead of showing the solution primarily as an experiment, frame it as a lesson.

Teacher → student.

## Contrast

Viewer is struggling / missing something → teacher has a method or lesson.

## Possible Structures

- I studied how this worked; here's the lesson.
- Someone solved this problem; here's how.
- I extracted the method; let me teach it.

## Tactical Steps

### A. Establish the pain.

### B. Introduce the solve as a set of learnings or a method.

### C. Explain why the process worked.

## Useful For

- Expertise
- Authority
- Education
- Consulting
- Selling knowledge

---

# 4. THE MAGICIAN

## Core Idea

Use a visual, sound, phrase, action, prop, or signature behavior as a **stun gun** for attention.

It tells the brain:

> Look at this.

## Examples of Mechanisms

- Unexpected visual
- Snap / motion
- Strange prop
- Costume switch
- Signature phrase
- Rhythmic movement
- Sudden sound
- Visually satisfying activity

## Visual Pacifier

A secondary activity can hold enough subconscious attention that the viewer remains engaged with the main content.

Examples of the principle:

- Doing makeup while talking
- Stacking objects
- Gameplay underneath another clip
- Building something by hand

## Important Rule

The Magician can be combined with any other hook archetype.

Example:

**Magician visual stun → Investigator reveal**

or:

**Magician visual stun → Teacher lesson**

---

# 5. THE INVESTIGATOR

## Core Idea

Reveal something the viewer does not know.

Unearth:

- Secret
- Finding
- Hidden mechanism
- Research insight
- Unexpected detail

## Contrast

Before:

> You don't know.

After:

> Now you know.

## Tactical Steps

### A. Signal that an unknown fact, secret, or discovery exists.

### B. Frame it against the viewer's current reality.

### C. Reveal enough to deepen curiosity before the explanation.

## Useful For

- Research
- AI / technology
- Marketing analysis
- Hidden systems
- Investigative storytelling
- Leading-edge category insights

---

# 6. THE CONTRARIAN

## Core Idea

Create contrast by directly challenging the common belief.

## Structure

> Everyone thinks A. The real answer is B.

or:

> You're being told A. I think B.

## Tactical Steps

1. Identify common assumption.
2. Identify meaningful opposing position.
3. State the conflict simply.
4. Support it in the content.

## Useful For

- Thought leadership
- Crowded categories
- Experts
- Opinion / insight content
- Debunking conventional wisdom

---

# SELECTING A HOOK

Choose the archetype based on the story's strongest source of contrast.

Ask:

- Is the story about the future? → Fortune Teller
- Is it a test? → Experimenter
- Is it a lesson? → Teacher
- Is the visual itself the scroll-stop? → Magician
- Is it a discovery? → Investigator
- Does it challenge accepted wisdom? → Contrarian

Multiple formats can be layered.

---

# VISUAL + VERBAL ALIGNMENT

A strong hook should align:

- What the viewer sees
- What the viewer hears
- What the viewer reads on-screen

Avoid a spoken hook about one subject while the visual communicates something unrelated.

The hero visual should make the subject instantly easier to understand.

---

# QUICK HOOK CHECK

Before publishing, ask:

1. What does the viewer believe at second zero?
2. What new idea does the hook introduce?
3. What is the contrast?
4. Can the viewer understand it within five seconds?
5. Is the visual reinforcing it?
6. Is there a reason to stay for the next line?
