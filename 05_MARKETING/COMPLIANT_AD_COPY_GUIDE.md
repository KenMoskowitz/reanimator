# COMPLIANT AD COPY GUIDE

This framework covers marketing and persuasive writing while avoiding unsupported or unverifiable claims.

---

# 1. HEADLINES ARE THE HOOK

The headline carries enormous weight.

It can determine whether anyone reads the rest.

Look for phrases that are:

- Emotional
- Unique
- Curiosity-driven
- Specific

Study what performs in the relevant niche.

High-performing marketers spend disproportionate effort on the headline because it affects everything downstream.

---

# 2. USE UNIQUE PROOF POINTS

Say what only you can credibly say.

Lead with:

- Specific achievement
- Rare experience
- Real-world evidence
- Original story
- Demonstrated result

People tend to trust evidence more than unsupported claims.

Defensible credibility comes from specifics.

---

# 3. SPEAK TO SOMEONE, NOT EVERYONE

Effective messages feel personal.

Be clear about:

- Who you are addressing
- Who you are not addressing

Exclusion can sharpen relevance.

Specificity beats vagueness.

Relevance often matters more than raw reach.

---

# 4. ALWAYS GIVE A REASON

People want justification for action.

Explain why:

- The offer exists
- Timing matters
- The CTA makes sense
- The opportunity is relevant now

Tie the reason to:

- Timing
- Value
- Context

Do not rely only on commands.

---

# 5. OWN THE FLAWS

Admit legitimate imperfections.

Transparency can increase trust.

A real limitation followed by the relevant advantage creates useful contrast.

Do not pretend the product or service is perfect.

The audience is already skeptical.

Honesty can become a proof mechanism.

---

# 6. SHOW, DON'T SAY

Do not merely write:

> You'll get more leads.

Show the moment.

Describe:

- What changes
- What the day looks like
- Sensory details
- Small wins
- Emotional relief
- Observable behavior

Specifics feel more believable.

Abstraction creates distance.

---

# 7. STATUS IS THE ULTIMATE CURRENCY

People often want more than the literal result.

They may want:

- Respect
- Recognition
- Competence
- Admiration
- Identity
- Belonging
- Social status

Ask what the practical result means socially and emotionally.

---

# 8. MAKE IT URGENT OR SCARCE

Urgency = limited time.

Scarcity = limited quantity.

Both can reduce hesitation when real.

Never fabricate:

- Deadlines
- Inventory
- Seats
- Price changes
- Availability

Fear of missing out only works ethically when there is genuinely something to miss.

---

# 9. SUBTLY SIGNAL AUTHORITY

Instead of saying:

> We're the best.

Let authority emerge from:

- Experience
- History
- Longevity
- Number served
- Relevant accomplishments
- Affiliations
- Demonstrated competence

Signal authority rather than loudly asserting it.

---

# 10. DON'T SKIP THE P.S.

The postscript can reinforce:

- CTA
- Main benefit
- Qualification
- Exclusion
- Deadline
- Link

A PPS can highlight one final important point.

---

# 11. DIRECT CALLS TO ACTION WIN

Do not assume the reader knows what to do.

Spell out the next step.

Use clear sequential action.

If the next step behaves exactly as promised, trust compounds.

Reduce friction.

---

# 12. WRITE LIKE YOU SPEAK

Use plain language.

The source guide recommends approximately a third-grade reading level for highly accessible copy.

Use:

- Simple words
- Short sentences
- Minimal friction
- Natural cadence

Remove anything that forces unnecessary rereading.

Good copy should feel easy to process.

---

# BONUS - USE HUMOR WHEN IT FITS

Humor earns attention when natural.

Do not force it.

Useful humor is:

- Specific
- Unexpected
- True to the voice

Test it.

When it fits, entertainment strengthens the rest of the message.
